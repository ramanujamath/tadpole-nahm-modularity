\opening{Dear Editors,}

I submit the manuscript \emph{Modular shifts of tadpole Nahm sums: classifications, companion identities, and boundary calculus} for consideration in [Journal name].

The paper concerns Nahm's problem for the tadpole matrices $T_r$: for which rational $(B,C)$ is the Nahm sum $F_{r,B,C}$ a modular function? Recent work of Milas--Wang, Shi--Wang and Chern--Tran--Wakhare proved modularity for particular shifts. The present paper addresses the converse question of completeness. Its main results are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Complete classifications} of the modular shifts in ranks $3,4,5,7,9$ (with $6,8,9,12,15$ elements), with no bound on the size or denominators of the shifts. Necessity uses the Vlasenko--Zwegers cusp expansion together with exact elimination. In ranks seven and nine, a criterion on highest homogeneous parts transfers a finite-field dimension bound to $\mathbb{Q}$.
\item \textbf{A conjectural description in all odd ranks}, consisting of $3(m+1)$ pairs in rank $2m+1$. We prove the sufficiency direction at the indices $k\in\{0,\dots,6,m\}$ via explicit Wronskian identities, so all conjectured pairs are modular through rank fifteen.
\item \textbf{A boundary calculus valid for all indices}, consisting of a finite kernel, an affine folding formula, and a minimal basis with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}[q]$. It reduces the remaining companion cases to an explicitly stated local identity.
\end{enumerate}

Parts of the proofs are computer-assisted. Appendix~B of the manuscript lists exactly the computations on which the proofs depend. The corresponding data and programs are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/ramanujamath/tadpole-nahm-modularity} and archived at doi:10.5281/zenodo.24522137. Where the manuscript relies on results that are currently available only as preprints, this is stated explicitly.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. [The author declares no competing interests.]
